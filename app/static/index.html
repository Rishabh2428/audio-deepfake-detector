    INTERPRETATION GUIDE

    What the measurements can tell you

    They describe the digital signal. They do not establish who or what produced it.

    01

    Decode and validate

    Full mode decodes WAV, FLAC, OGG/Opus, AIFF, MP3, M4A/MP4, AAC, WMA, and WebM locally. It checks duration, sample rate, channels, file size, and finite samples before analysis. No uploaded audio is retained.

    02

    Canonicalize audio

    Channels are averaged to mono and resampled to 16 kHz. The report keeps the original rate, channel count, format, and stereo fold-down level so input changes are visible.

    03

    Time-domain DSP

    RMS and peak levels, DC offset, clipping fraction, and relative low-energy-frame fraction describe amplitude and recording quality.

    04

    STFT · CQT-style analysis

    25 ms Hann STFT frames and 10 ms hops produce spectral centroid, rolloff, flatness, entropy, flux, and PSD. A log-frequency filter bank adds CQT-style bands; it is clearly marked as an approximation, not a full CQT.

    05

    Speech-quality gates

    Short, quiet, clipped, anti-phase, or strongly tonal inputs receive warnings. DTMF is a tone sequence rather than speech, so the voice classifier withholds its class result.

    06

    Adaptive ML from trusted labels

    Human/AI labels are added only after explicit confirmation or dataset import. Compare Logistic Regression, SVM, Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, and a small MLP. Prediction history is never silently treated as ground truth.

    07

    Training and evaluation

    Training uses explicit human/synthetic labels, at least 16 recordings, and at least 4 recording groups per class. A deterministic grouped 25% holdout reports accuracy, balanced accuracy, ROC AUC, confusion matrix, and group counts.

    08

    Decision policy

    The 1–11 synthetic evidence index is mapped from an uncalibrated model output. The user-selected threshold controls the label. It is not a probability; speech-quality blockers and non-speech tones stay INCONCLUSIVE.

    09

    Pitch · HNR · jitter · shimmer

    Autocorrelation estimates F0, voiced ratio, and a harmonic-to-noise proxy. Jitter and shimmer are short-frame proxies, not clinical voice measurements. They depend on speech content and recording conditions.

    10

    MFCC · LFCC · CQCC-style

    Log-filter-bank cepstra are summarized alongside linear-frequency and mel-frequency cepstra. The CQCC-style path uses geometrically spaced STFT filters; it does not claim a full constant-Q transform.

    12

    Phase-aware and spectral cues

    Residual STFT phase, group-delay outliers, spectral slope, entropy, crest, flux, and band energy are measured and shown. Their influence varies with codecs, microphones, edits, and content.

    13

    Measurement explanations

    Explain DSP evidence locally, or explicitly opt in to send an allowlisted set of measurements to Gemini or OpenAI. No audio, filename, history, model score, or verdict is sent.

    11

    Input quality score

    The 0–100 indicator summarizes technical checks such as level, clipping, silence, duration, and stereo cancellation. It is not a detector confidence, class probability, or measure of model accuracy.

    !

    Current boundary

    The model learns only explicitly labeled recordings in this local project. Small or imbalanced data, uncertain labels, speaker/generator overlap, codec differences, and new synthesis methods can produce misleading scores. External models are listed for research but are not installed here.

    METHOD · FEATURE GUIDE

    Plain-English feature guide

    What each measurement means and why it matters

    F0 / Pitch

    How fast vocal-fold vibration appears to repeat; track its movement over time.

    Estimated from short-frame autocorrelation.

    Jitter

    Small changes in estimated pitch period from one voiced frame to the next.

    Proxy only; low is not automatically fake.

    Shimmer

    Frame-level loudness variation across nearby voiced frames.

    Microphone and speech dependent.

    HNR

    Compares periodic harmonic structure with noise-like energy.

    A proxy; very clean is not automatically synthetic.

    Spectral centroid

    A rough spectral “center of mass”; higher values mean more high-frequency energy.

    Microphone dependent.

    Spectral rolloff

    Frequency below which most measured spectral energy lies.

    Useful for comparing bandwidth changes.

    Spectral flatness

    How tone-like versus noise-like the spectrum is.

    Compare patterns, not one value.

    Spectral flux

    How quickly the spectrum changes from frame to frame.

    Describes temporal texture.

    Voiced ratio

    Fraction of short frames that appear pitch-bearing.

    Speech-content dependent.

    Spectral entropy

    How spread out spectral energy is; a description of complexity.

    Useful as a pattern, not a verdict.

    Spectral slope

    How spectral energy falls or rises with frequency.

    Affected by microphone and channel.

    Spectral crest

    Compares the strongest spectral peak with average energy.

    Compare with other features.

    RMS mean / variation

    Tracks signal energy and how much loudness changes across frames.

    Recording-level dependent.

    MFCC / LFCC

    Cepstral summaries from mel-spaced and linear-spaced STFT filter banks.

    Compact timbre descriptors; not standalone evidence.

    CQCC-style

    Log-frequency filter-bank cepstra with approximately constant-Q spacing.

    CQT-style approximation, not a full constant-Q transform.

    Phase-aware STFT

    Measures residual phase change after removing the expected FFT-bin advance.

    Sensitive to codec, edits, and recording conditions.

    STFT spectrogram

    Shows how signal energy is distributed across frequency as the recording changes over time.

    Relative display only; patterns do not prove whether speech is human or generated.

    Envelope modulation

    Describes slow changes in short-frame loudness, including modulation energy in the 2–8 Hz range.

    Speech-content and recording dependent; shown for inspection, not used by the saved model.

    STFT uses 25 ms Hann frames and 10 ms hops. Choose Basic or Extended in Train model; each report lists the exact feature set used.
